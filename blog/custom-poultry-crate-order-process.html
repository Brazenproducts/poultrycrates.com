The Custom Poultry Crate Order Process: From Spec to Delivery

Poultrycrates Editorial • October 2026

Stock crates rarely fit a poultry operation perfectly. A crate that is an inch too tall wastes trailer cube on every load; one that does not tile cleanly onto your pallet pattern loses capacity on every shipment. Custom crates solve this, but many procurement teams avoid them because the ordering process feels opaque. It need not be. Here is how a well-run custom poultry crate order actually works.

Step 1: Define the Application

Before any dimension is drawn, document how the crate will be used. Live bird transport, processed product distribution, and cold-chain handling each drive different ventilation, drainage, and strength requirements. Capture your load weight, stacking height, temperature range, wash-line compatibility, and whether the crate runs through automated handling. This application profile is the foundation of every downstream decision.

Step 2: Specify Dimensions and Structure

With the application defined, specify internal and external dimensions, wall thickness, ventilation pattern, and load ratings. This is where custom sizing pays off—rather than forcing your operation around a stock footprint, you size the crate to your pallet pattern and trailer cube. Elipacko works from your spec sheet to design PP poultry crates and boxes from Elipacko that match your exact handling environment. A good supplier will push back where a spec is structurally unsound and propose alternatives.

Step 3: Prototype and Validate

Never commit to a production run without validating a prototype in your actual environment. Run sample crates through a real cold-chain cycle, a wash line, and your automated handling. Check stacking stability under load, ventilation performance with live birds or product, and dimensional fit on your pallets. Catching a problem at the prototype stage costs almost nothing; catching it after a full production run is expensive.

  • Fit test: confirm palletization and trailer cube in practice, not on paper.
  • Load test: verify stacking strength at your real operating temperature and humidity.
  • Sanitation test: run the crate through your wash line to confirm durability.
  • Handling test: validate grip and conveyance on automated lines.

Step 4: Confirm Compliance

For poultry, FDA material compliance is non-negotiable. Elipacko manufactures with FDA-compliant resin rated for direct food contact, so crates meet food-safety material requirements out of the gate. Confirm the resin certification and any customer-specific or retailer compliance documentation during this phase so there are no surprises at delivery.

Step 5: Tooling and Production

Custom crates require tooling, which carries a one-time cost and lead time. Once tooling is complete, production runs are fast and repeatable, and reorders skip the tooling step entirely. Factor the tooling investment into your ROI model—it is amortized across the full production volume and every future reorder, so at scale its per-unit impact is minimal.

Step 6: Lead Times and Logistics

Elipacko operates as a US-available direct manufacturer with production in Thailand and Vietnam, both under a 0% anti-dumping duty structure that avoids the surcharges on China-origin PP. Plan lead times to include tooling, production, and ocean freight for the initial order; reorders are faster. US domestic manufacturing is in development, which will further shorten North American lead times. For operations needing produce and field packaging alongside poultry, the broader agriculture packaging solutions catalog consolidates these under one supplier relationship.

Making the Process Work

The difference between a smooth custom order and a frustrating one is preparation. Arrive with a clear application profile and spec sheet, insist on prototype validation, confirm compliance early, and plan lead times realistically. Done well, a custom poultry crate becomes a precisely fitted asset that returns value across hundreds of trips—far outperforming any compromise stock box.
